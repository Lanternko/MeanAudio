## Q23 — Main review and comments for the authors

This paper advances unsupervised text-to-music generation by introducing a prompt-consistency condition into the music generation model. Both quantitative and qualitative evaluation results indicate that the proposed method is preferred over the baseline.

The introduction and related works section is solid in raising problems that the authors would like to resolve.

The methodology section seems appropriate with the supporting logic behind it. It was very clear how the authors made each decision with supporting knowledge, such as not filtering out prompts with a low prompt consistency score, and an ablation study on how each component of the proposed pipeline is necessary. Also, the training setting described in Section 4, where the authors adopt a fully unsupervised TTM scenario by using LP-MusicCaps-generated captions rather than the existing music tags in MTG-Jamendo, further supports the practical applicability of the proposed method

One minor question that I have is from section 3.2, where the authors have mentioned that auto-generated captions may not be consistent due to the ambiguity of the music captioning task and the potential errors made by the captioning model from lines 191-194. This is a pretty strong claim, even though this problem is well known throughout the field, so a citation would have been helpful.

Also, the proposed conditioning method improves the baseline both quantitatively and qualitatively, but the quantitative result seems slightly less significant compared to the baseline. Even the quantitative result supports the claim, but because it is hard to know who these 18 participants are without the demographics information, providing this information or increasing the number of participants in a small amount might have been helpful.

Analyzing the effect of q scores at inference time brings an interesting claim, which is that even with a high prompt consistency score, the model might still interpret differently, and a q score over 5 does not seem to bring a clear direction to the TTM model. This could also bring up the question, does the q score actually matter, but overall, bringing this into a paper is still a good direction, bringing future research direction for researchers.
